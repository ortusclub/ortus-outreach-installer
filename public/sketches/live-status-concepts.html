Live Status — Concept Sketches

Three approaches to "what is the campaign doing RIGHT NOW", each designed so an impatient operator glancing across the room can tell at a glance whether the system is working or stuck. All sketches share the existing monochrome command-deck aesthetic — gold accent, hairline borders, Bebas display. Click a state below each concept to see how it responds.

A Broadcast cinematic banner — read across a room

Best for: someone who wants to glance at the screen from their desk and just see "is it doing something?". Massive Bebas display label fills the visual field. Animated dots ... never stop, even when state hasn't changed in a while — proves the loop is alive. For waits, the gold bar slides as a countdown; for indeterminate steps (loading a page) it animates as a back-and-forth indeterminate pulse.

Trade-off: takes the most vertical space. Probably wants its own dedicated panel under Live Status.

B Cockpit circular ring — precise, technical

Best for: operators who like instrumentation. Big circular gauge with the countdown number inside, ring depletes clockwise as time passes. For non-timed actions, ring becomes a slow rotating arc instead — still visibly moving so the operator knows it's alive. Right side shows action label + structured metadata (lead, sender, mode). Green pulsing dot = "loop is still ticking".

Trade-off: compact and information-dense, but less dramatic than A. Better for power users than for someone glancing from the kitchen.

C Ticker terminal-style heartbeat strip

Best for: operators who want a single thin status bar that's always present, like a deployment log. Mono font, single-line, pulsing green "LIVE" dot, animated trailing dots that NEVER stop. Dedicated countdown column on the right for waits. Optional second row with a thin progress bar for the current step.

Trade-off: least visually loud. If the operator is across the room they may not see the dots moving. Best when the dashboard is the active window.
