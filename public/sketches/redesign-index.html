A · Mission Control

Fly the outreach.

SpaceX Dragon console meets Bloomberg terminal.

Near-black surface, phosphor-green as data (never decoration). Accounts are channel strips with live waveforms. Numbers timestamped to the second. Bottom scrolls like a stock ticker.

Feels like something flown, not clicked. Best for operators who want to watch every lane at once.

B · Editorial

Publish the edition.

A Monocle magazine that happens to run LinkedIn campaigns.

Warm cream paper, single oxblood accent, Fraunces serif throughout. Sections become articles with drop caps, standfirsts, pull-quotes. Launch is a letterpress dispatch button.

Stops feeling like software, starts feeling like you're publishing an edition. The biggest tonal leap from today — nobody in outreach looks like this.

C · Physical Device

Hold the hardware.

The app feels like a piece of hardware sitting on your desk.

Linear × Arc × Teenage Engineering. Real depth. Panels have weight, knurled steppers, analog dials, buttons that depress. Electric-blue accent used for state.

Micro-motion everywhere — numbers roll, panels breathe, the run bar pulses. Feels alive without being busy. The most "app-like" of the three; ages best.

How to read these.

Each sketch keeps the current information architecture exactly as it is — Settings → Rate → Sheet → Accounts → Templates → Launch → Live Status. Only the register changes: the typography, the metaphor, the way a running campaign feels under your hands.

A (Mission Control) is closest in spirit to today's monochrome deck — a natural evolution. B (Editorial) is the boldest tonal leap and probably the most ownable position. C (Physical) is the most conventionally "app"-like and likely the safest bet commercially.

Pick one, pick two, or ask for a hybrid — the sketches are a forcing function for the decision, not the final build.
